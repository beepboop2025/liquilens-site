India bank and NBFC risk / evidence explained

Are Indian microfinance lenders under stress?

The retained evidence warrants lender-by-lender scrutiny, but it does not establish a current industry-wide stress verdict. In the June 2026 filings covered here, Suryoday, Utkarsh and ESAF reported gross NPA ratios of 6.50%, 5.90% and 5.40%. Their quarterly GNPA ratios were flat, down 1.70 percentage points and flat, respectively. These bank-wide ratios are not microfinance-only default rates.

What these numbers answer—and what they leave open

They show the reported stock of non-performing advances relative to each filing’s denominator. They do not show this month’s collection efficiency, borrower overlap, household indebtedness, the performance of new loan vintages or the funding terms offered to an NBFC-MFI.

ESAF’s retained GNPA ratio was flat quarter over quarter and 2.10 percentage points lower year over year. Calling this particular accepted observation a fresh jump would be inaccurate. A different time window can tell a different story.

The next evidence to request

Look for microfinance loan share, borrower and geographic concentration, collection-efficiency definitions, slippages, write-offs and portfolio vintages. Keep bank-wide asset quality separate from segment-specific figures. Ask whether reported collection efficiency excludes overdue accounts or includes arrears recovered.

For an NBFC-MFI, add borrowing maturity, lender concentration, undrawn facilities and actual funding cost. This retained set does not support a league table of NBFCs unable to obtain bank funding. Missing financing evidence remains missing.

What this evidence cannot establish

  • This is a selected set of accepted public filings, not a census of Indian lenders or a ranking of deposit safety.
  • A change in an NPA ratio is measured in percentage points. Recoveries, loan growth, write-offs and sales can all affect ratios; the ratio alone does not identify the cause.
  • The current model card does not establish an out-of-time validated false-positive rate. Historical replay evidence is not a validated probability of failure.
  • A June reporting period remains June evidence even when it is retrieved or reviewed in October. A newer unreviewed filing may exist.
