Does tight dollar funding mean a bank will fail?

No. Dollar funding conditions describe a market or funding channel; they do not establish that a particular bank will fail. Seiche’s retained 2 October 2026 SOFR–IORB spread is −2 bp, while LiquiLens’s separate AU filing ends 30 June and reports a 2.10% gross NPA ratio. Those observations answer different questions on different dates. Neither can be converted into the other product’s verdict.

What connects funding and institution risk?

A lender that depends on a particular source of short-term funding can be sensitive to the price and availability of that funding. The transmission depends on its balance sheet, collateral, currency, maturity, counterparties and access to liquidity. A broad US-dollar rate cannot identify those exposures by itself.

A useful institution review therefore adds funding concentration, liability maturity, liquid assets, capital, asset quality and event evidence. Domestic Indian filings and US-dollar market measures have different scopes; a causal link needs institution-specific support.

Use one product for each part of the question

Seiche provides funding-market context. LiquiLens provides dated institution disclosures and their gaps. Undertow describes market-depth and position-size evidence if the question includes selling assets. Keep the three outputs separate and cite each observation date.

None of these readings alone authorizes credit, guarantees deposits or supplies a calibrated failure probability. Where the balance-sheet exposure or validation evidence is missing, the correct conclusion is that the failure question is unresolved.

What this evidence cannot establish

  • This is a selected set of accepted public filings, not a census of Indian lenders or a ranking of deposit safety.
  • A change in an NPA ratio is measured in percentage points. Recoveries, loan growth, write-offs and sales can all affect ratios; the ratio alone does not identify the cause.
  • The current model card does not establish an out-of-time validated false-positive rate. Historical replay evidence is not a validated probability of failure.
  • A June reporting period remains June evidence even when it is retrieved or reviewed in October. A newer unreviewed filing may exist.
  • Correlation or simultaneous movement does not establish a causal chain from dollar funding to a particular bank failure.
