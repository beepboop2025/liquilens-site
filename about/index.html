About

A radar built by one stubborn reader.

LiquiLens is an early warning system for bank and lender failure. It reads what institutions and regulators publish, including filings, funding structure and market signals, and flags names showing signs of distress. In parts of the historical record, those signals appeared years before ratings moved. Every call lands on a tamper evident record, and the misses are published next to the hits.

The founder

LiquiLens is built by Mrinal, a lawyer who taught herself to build software and who reads bank filings the way lawyers read evidence. The work began with Indian NBFC diagnostics after the IL&FS, DHFL and Yes Bank records showed how much warning sat in public filings. It then extended into US bank work and now into market-specific studies across additional countries and institution types. What travels is the evidence discipline: chronology, provenance, coverage limits and published misses. Disclosures, regulations and fitted models stay specific to each market.
