Validate the claim, not the presentation.

LiquiLens publishes construction-point-in-time status, named misses, source boundaries and explicit eligibility flags. That lets a reviewer separate a sealed execution pass from evidence completeness, independent validation and production permission.

Four questions before a performance claim

Was information actually available then?

Construction-PIT diagnostics approximate or reconstruct availability. They are labelled rather than presented as genuine prospective history.

What did the system miss?

Named misses and unresolved mappings stay in the record. A headline recall figure never erases them.

What is authorised?

Backtest eligibility, real-money eligibility and production execution are separate booleans, not inferred from a completed run.

Decision boundary

A sealed run proves that a defined process executed. It does not make the research definition complete, convert reconstructed history into prospective evidence, or authorise production decisions.

Questions model reviewers ask

Are the current LiquiLens historical diagnostics backtest eligible?

No. The current public market records explicitly set validated-backtest eligible and real-money eligible to false.

Why publish misses?

Named misses reveal where public-data coverage or the screening rule did not produce an adequate warning, which is necessary for model-risk review.
