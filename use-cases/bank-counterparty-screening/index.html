Use case · credit and treasury teams

Review the counterparty before the limit meeting.

LiquiLens turns public filings, funding fragility, regulatory thresholds and market pressure into a dated review queue for covered banks, NBFCs and MFIs. It helps a human decide what to investigate; it does not make a credit decision.

The workflow

1. Establish coverage

Confirm the legal name is on the current public board. Absence is reported as uncovered, never low risk.

2. Inspect the factors

Read the filing date, funding structure, asset-quality movement, regulatory distance and market layer separately.

3. Record the review

Attach the source payload and as-of date to the human limit decision. Do not cite the screen beyond its published evidence status.

Good fit and wrong fit

Use LiquiLens forDo not use it for
Prioritising covered lender counterparties for reviewDeclaring that an institution will fail
Checking whether public evidence deteriorated between meetingsReplacing a licensed credit rating or internal due diligence
Preserving a dated public-data record beside a human decisionInferring calm from a missing, stale or uncovered name

Decision boundary

LiquiLens is research screening, not investment advice, a credit rating or a supervisory conclusion. Customer-specific exposure and private-book conclusions require validation inside the customer's controlled environment.

Questions risk teams ask

Does a LiquiLens flag mean a counterparty will fail?

No. A flag prioritises a covered institution for review; it is not a default prediction or credit rating.

Can LiquiLens screen every bank or NBFC?

No. Coverage is limited to institutions with sufficiently current, vetted public evidence. Uncovered names remain explicitly unavailable.
