*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. It examines Nedungadi Bank through a filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT record. The default date is 2002-11-02; the first action-zone period-end is 1999-03-31, with a knowledge-time proxy of 1999-05-30. Similar mechanisms do not imply the same outcome at another institution.*

The narrow answer to the desk question is Nedungadi Bank’s disclosed capital position. The first recorded qualifying signal was a CRAR breach in the RBI action zone, attached to FY1999. That is a filing-period signal: a reconstruction of when a disclosed condition could have become available under the dossier’s publication convention. It is not a market-price signal, because no market-derived measure establishes the case. Nor is it a model derivation that can convert the breach into a failure probability.

The distinction is decisive. The replay measures a lead of 41 months from its availability proxy to the recorded default. It does not show that every relevant observer possessed, interpreted, or acted on the information at that time. It does not prove that the capital breach caused the default. It says only that, among the available lenses, capital was the first recorded action-zone pressure point.

## The record before the event

Nedungadi Bank entered the first RBI PCA/SAF action zone at the 1999-03-31 period-end, designated FY1999, because of CRAR. Where no explicit publication clock is present, the record uses period-end plus 60 days. That yields the 1999-05-30 knowledge-time proxy. The institution’s default is recorded on 2002-11-02.

This is explicitly labelled `PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT`. It is not eligible for validated backtesting or real-money use; it has no bitemporal input contract; and the dossier says its lead times are optimistic. A reporting date, a proxy availability date, and the moment at which a depositor, investor, supervisor, or counterparty understands a filing are different events. The 41-month interval must therefore be read as a construction result, not as a contemporaneous trading or supervisory record.

The case file is available in the [Nedungadi Bank replay](https://liquilens.in/replay/nedungadi-bank/). The wider rule set and roster are on the [Failure Radar board](https://api.liquilens.in/api/failure-radar/board). Neither source makes the historical replay a present-tense finding.

## What the lenses saw

The PCA lens recorded CRAR as Nedungadi’s first action-zone breach. The funding lens was not scoreable: it has no first signal and no lead time for this institution. That absence is a missing-data disclosure, not a favorable funding conclusion. The funding lens can only observe institutions that disclose a liability series.

The historical summaries illustrate why the two lenses should not be collapsed into one story. Of 15 failed institutions, 5 entered an action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. Of the same 15 failures, 10 had liability disclosures; the funding signal fired first for 4, with a median lead of 38 months. Those figures show that funding and action-zone signals can arrive separately. They do not establish that one lens universally leads the other, and they cannot resolve Nedungadi’s funding condition without the missing liability series.

The hazard work is also diagnostic rather than promotable evidence. Its leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with a confidence interval from 0.338 to 1.0. The heuristic score is 0.799 on the same held-out rows. The temporal test AUC is 0.645, below its 0.65 gate. The panel contains 205 rows, 9 events, and 27 institutions, while 179 censored or unusable rows are excluded. The [historical validation record](https://api.liquilens.in/api/failure-radar/validation) is a limit disclosure, not a performance certificate.

## Why the warning mattered

An RBI action-zone breach matters because it identifies a regulatory threshold tied, in this case, to capital. The defensible inference is not that the threshold supplied a default clock. It is that a breach can identify a balance-sheet constraint before a recorded failure, and that this constraint deserves investigation alongside asset quality, earnings, funding, management response, and disclosure reliability.

For Nedungadi, the warning mattered because it was concrete and independently specified: CRAR, not an after-the-event composite score and not a price-based alarm. The record supports a pressure-chain hypothesis in which constrained loss-absorption capacity may make adverse developments harder to accommodate. But the dossier does not supply the intervening causal history needed to prove that sequence. It provides no scoreable funding confirmation and no market-price evidence for the institution.

That restraint is the point. A filing-period breach can be useful without being a forecast. A market-price signal would instead describe repricing in traded prices; none is established here. A model output would be a derivation from its inputs and assumptions; it cannot repair unavailable funding evidence or turn proxy timing into observed knowledge.

## The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat any inflated version of the thesis. A capital threshold breach can be an early, noisy regulatory classification rather than a reliable marker of eventual failure. A lender may restore capital, reduce risk, improve earnings, obtain support, or otherwise leave the pressure zone. The 41-month lead leaves substantial room for paths other than default.

The construction itself could overstate the apparent advance warning. The 60-day filing convention is a proxy when an explicit publication clock is absent. The dossier says lead times are optimistic and denies validated-backtest and real-money eligibility. Its temporal diagnostic does not clear its stated gate. Its PCA summary is a public-disclosure replay of RBI tripwires, not a causal account of each institution’s failure.

The missing funding series is equally damaging to an overconfident thesis. The record cannot demonstrate a deposit or wholesale-funding event for Nedungadi, cannot rank capital against funding for this case, and cannot establish that capital was the only important vulnerability. The correct conclusion remains narrower: CRAR was the first recorded qualifying lens, not the sole explanation.

## The next falsifiable test

The capital-first interpretation would weaken if prospectively timestamped disclosures showed that the CRAR breach was not available by the assumed proxy date. It would weaken further if comparable action-zone breaches regularly resolved without subsequent deterioration, while institutions without such breaches displayed equal or greater recorded distress.

A stronger test requires actual publication clocks, preserved amended-file vintages, distinct capital and liability series, and outcomes observed without retrofitting the rule. Until then, the 41-month figure is an investigative clue. It is not a calibrated probability, a trade signal, or investment advice.
