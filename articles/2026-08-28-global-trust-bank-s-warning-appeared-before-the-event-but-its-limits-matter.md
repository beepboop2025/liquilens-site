*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. It examines Global Trust Bank using a construction-PIT record. The historical disclosure sequence, current market observations, and current board are different clocks and must not be treated as one signal.*

The narrow thesis is that Global Trust Bank first showed observable stress through reported balance-sheet weakness: capital adequacy and net non-performing assets crossed the replayed action-zone screen before default. The funding lens did not offer a rival early call because it was unscoreable. That is not evidence that funding was sound. It is missing evidence.

The more important qualification is the fraud-masked designation. A filing-period threshold can identify strain in reported data; it cannot verify that reported data describe the underlying institution. Nor should a later market-price signal be read back into a filing replay. And a model derivation is neither of those things: it is a rule constructed from inputs, assumptions and availability conventions. The warning therefore warranted investigation. It did not establish a forecastable path to failure.

## The record before the event

Global Trust Bank’s first replayed action-zone entry was at the period ending 2002-03-31, identified as FY2002. The breached measures were CRAR and NNPA. The record assigns a knowledge-time proxy of 2002-05-30 and a lead of 25 months to the 2004-07-24 default date.

That sequence is a filing-period signal, not proof of what supervisors knew privately or what the balance sheet economically contained. The PCA lens is a public-disclosure replay of the RBI’s own PCA/SAF tripwires. Its summary covers 15 failed institutions; 5 entered the action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. Global Trust Bank’s lead is therefore an observation inside a small historical set, not a timetable that can be exported to another institution.

The historical construction uses an explicit publication clock where available; otherwise it applies period end plus 60 days. That availability convention is deliberately conservative relative to simply assuming contemporaneous knowledge, but it remains a proxy. The dossier says lead times are optimistic and the record lacks a bitemporal input contract. Those limits matter before any apparent early warning is called predictive. The underlying constraints are set out in the [Failure Radar validation record](https://api.liquilens.in/api/failure-radar/validation).

## What the lenses saw

The primary balance-sheet lens saw CRAR and NNPA breaches. This is the core filing-period observation: reported capital capacity and reported asset quality simultaneously met the action-zone conditions. It supports a concern that disclosed loss-absorption capacity could be under pressure if asset-quality deterioration required recognition. It does not reveal unreported exposures, establish causation, or reconstruct management decisions.

The funding lens saw no scoreable series. Global Trust Bank has no first funding signal and no funding lead time in the record. Across the failed-institution funding summary, liability disclosures exist for 10 of 15 institutions, and the funding signal fired first for 4. The lens can only assess institutions that disclose a liability series. An unscoreable result is consequently an observation boundary, not an all-clear.

The fraud-masked tag supplies a third, distinct category of evidence. It is not a contemporaneous market-price signal and not a ratio crossing. It states the key failure mode: filings later shown falsified can look compliant, or only partly impaired, to a threshold engine. The forensic screen owns that problem. The balance-sheet screen may still have found a useful symptom, but it could not authenticate the numbers it received.

## Why the warning mattered

The warning mattered because it converted diffuse concern into an auditable escalation condition. A CRAR and NNPA breach gave an analyst a reason to ask whether reported capital could absorb further recognition of asset-quality weakness, whether disclosures were sufficiently reliable, and whether liabilities required separate investigation.

That is a weaker and more defensible claim than saying the screen predicted default. The recorded 25-month lead created time for diligence, not a countdown. It also did not make funding stress visible: the relevant series was unavailable to the lens. And fraud masking means even the observed balance-sheet sequence may understate, distort, or incompletely represent the condition it appears to measure.

India’s broader evidence should be read with the same restraint. The construction-PIT diagnostic covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. Yet it is explicitly not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and not supported by a bitemporal input contract. The [market evidence index](https://api.liquilens.in/api/evidence/markets) labels it a filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT diagnostic, rather than a prospective performance record.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat any stronger version of this thesis. Global Trust Bank was fraud-masked. If the disclosed record was unreliable, CRAR and NNPA breaches may be imperfect symptoms rather than a clean diagnosis of the actual balance sheet. The replay establishes ordering in available public data, not the full economic chronology of the institution’s distress.

The validation sample also does not justify broad certainty. Only 5 of 15 failed institutions entered the PCA action zone first. The funding lens cannot corroborate or challenge this case because Global Trust Bank was unscoreable. A missing liability series leaves a meaningful part of the pressure chain unobserved.

Nor can a construction-PIT result become a current investment claim by repetition. The proxy clock, lack of bitemporal inputs, and optimistic lead-time warning prevent that move. The strongest warranted conclusion is only that reported CRAR and NNPA deterioration supplied a legitimate trigger for deeper scrutiny before the event. It was insufficient to infer default, dismiss funding risk, or overcome a forensic-data problem.

## The next falsifiable test

The thesis would weaken if fresh, vetted disclosures showed that institutions flagged by reported capital, asset-quality, or market-distance conditions did not sustain deterioration while comparable unflagged institutions did. It would weaken further if prospectively timestamped disclosure records failed to preserve the observed order between warning and later stress.

For this replay specifically, the claim would be challenged if a forensic review found that the CRAR and NNPA crossings had no durable relationship to balance-sheet pressure once reliable information was available. Conversely, a prospective record in which independently verified deterioration precedes weaker resilience would support the narrower use of these screens as escalation tools. Neither result follows automatically from a historical replay.

## Follow the pressure chain

The useful chain is deliberately incomplete: reported asset-quality and capital pressure; lower apparent capacity to absorb losses; deeper verification of the reported balance sheet; and, where disclosed, separate examination of liabilities, market repricing, and forensic indicators. The chain stops at missing evidence. Global Trust Bank’s funding series was unscoreable, and fraud masking means ratio analysis cannot close the gap.

LiquiLens covers **institution and lender balance-sheet risk**. [Seiche](https://api.seiche.info/api/overview) covers **system dollar-funding capacity**. A system read does not prove an institution-level failure path, and a market-liquidity observation does not validate a filing-period ratio.
