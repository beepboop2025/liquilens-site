United Western Bank’s first disclosed fault line was asset quality, not funding

The replay places an NNPA action-zone breach before United Western Bank’s recorded default, while the scoreable funding lens produced no first signal. That sequence is informative, but its timing is a construction-PIT availability proxy, not a tradable forecast.

1414 words · institution risk · open evidence

*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. It examines disclosures associated with United Western Bank before its recorded default on 2006-09-02. Similar mechanisms do not imply the same outcome at another institution.*

United Western Bank offers a narrow answer to the desk question: the earliest disclosed pressure point in this replay was asset quality. The record identifies an NNPA breach in the PCA action zone for the period ended 2001-03-31, assigns a knowledge-time proxy of 2001-05-30, and measures a 63-month lead to default. The funding module was scoreable, yet it produced no first signal.

That is a sequencing observation, not a claim that an NNPA threshold determines failure. A filing-period signal says what a reported balance-sheet measure showed at period end. An availability proxy estimates when that disclosure may have been knowable. A market-price signal would show separate contemporaneous repricing. A model derivation summarizes a rule or statistical relationship built from a dataset. This case supplies the first, proxies the second, does not supply the third, and should not be enlarged into the fourth.

The record before the event

The public-disclosure replay identifies United Western Bank’s first PCA action-zone entry in FY2001, for the period ending 2001-03-31. The stated breach is NNPA. The case is not marked fraud-masked. The replay assigns its knowledge-time proxy to 2001-05-30 and measures 63 months to the default date of 2006-09-02.

Those facts establish an observed order, not inevitability or a complete causal history. The record does not show every later management decision, creditor response, liability movement, recovery path, or shock. Nor does it establish that outside users possessed the same information at the proxy date. A reporting date is not itself a public-knowledge date.

The funding result is limited rather than reassuring. The module was scoreable, but no first signal is recorded. That means the available liability-disclosure lens did not provide the first recorded alert. It does not certify stable funding, exclude later funding stress, or demonstrate calm. The funding lens only sees institutions that disclose a liability series, so missing liability disclosure remains a material boundary rather than a benign result.

What the lenses saw

The PCA lens saw the asset-quality action-zone breach first. In the historical validation summary, 15 failed institutions are included; 5 entered an action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. This is a public-disclosure replay of RBI PCA/SAF tripwires, not a complete account of information available to a lender, depositor, supervisor, or market participant. The underlying framework can be inspected through the failure-radar board and historical validation record.

The funding lens did not fire first here. Across the same summary, 10 of 15 failed institutions have liability disclosures; a funding signal fired first for 4, with a median lead of 38 months. Funding can therefore lead in observable cases. But the result does not show that funding should have led in this case, because observation depends on disclosure and the available series is not a complete map of all funding channels.

A broader India diagnostic covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports that 88.9% of non-fraud failures were flagged, with a median lead of 21.5 months. Its evidence is period-end-proxy construction PIT. It is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and has no bitemporal input contract. The evidence index is useful for inspection, but a construction diagnostic is not evidence of executable performance.

Why the warning mattered

The warning mattered because it located disclosed pressure in asset quality before the replay recorded a funding alert. That differs sharply from saying funding was already broken. An asset-quality tripwire can identify a balance-sheet area requiring scrutiny before losses, capital pressure, earnings weakness, funding sensitivity, or confidence effects are fully visible in the available record.

The case supports only a modest proposition: the balance sheet that feels disclosed stress first may be the one whose asset-quality threshold has already been crossed, even if another lens is silent. It does not establish a universal ordering among assets, capital, funding, and market pricing. It also does not turn NNPA into a calibrated probability of default.

A market-price signal remains a separate category of evidence. The current market layer uses Yahoo Finance daily closes, auto-adjusted, and is fresh as of 2026-09-28. But market-derived display, admission, model-gate, and tier use lack approval; admitted canonical records are 0/25. Clock and knowledge fields do not authorize publication or tier use. Those governance limits prevent current market data from being retrofitted into this historical case.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis. This may be retrospective pattern recognition: because United Western Bank later defaulted, an earlier breach can appear more decisive after the fact than it was when disclosed. The replay uses an explicit publication clock when present; otherwise it uses period end plus 60 days. Its evidence states that lead times are optimistic. Actual availability could differ from the assigned proxy.

The threshold may also not have been the most informative signal in a fully reconstructed record. Funding could have deteriorated through channels not captured by the disclosed liability series. Other facts may have been known to creditors or supervisors. The absence of a first funding signal is therefore missing explanatory power, not affirmative evidence of resilience.

The model record adds restraint. The temporal diagnostic reports an AUC of 0.645 against a 0.65 gate and does not pass. The hazard result reports AUC of 0.752, while the comparison heuristic reports 0.799 on the same held-out rows. These are model derivations and diagnostics, not grounds to promote the replay into a validated prediction engine.

Threshold systems also cannot uncover concealed facts. The PCA note says filings later shown falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns those cases. United Western Bank is not fraud-masked, but that limitation still constrains the inference.

The next falsifiable test

The thesis should fail if prospectively clocked filings show that action-zone asset-quality breaches do not precede later balance-sheet stress more reliably than competing signals after disclosure becomes available. It should also fail if usable liability disclosures show funding signals consistently arriving first in comparable cases, or if bitemporal validation removes the apparent lead.

A meaningful test needs explicit publication times, preserved revisions, rules fixed before outcomes are known, and retained missing-data exclusions. Until then, the 63-month lead is an informative replay measure, not an estimate of what an investor could have earned or avoided.

Follow the pressure chain

LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. Undertow covers market liquidity and executable exit capacity. These are distinct research boundaries, not interchangeable labels.

For this case, begin with the disclosed asset-quality threshold; then ask what the record can and cannot say about capital, earnings, and funding; then separate institutional facts from market exit conditions. The United Western replay belongs to the first task. The Seiche overview and Undertow board address different questions and cannot be fused into one unsupported verdict.
