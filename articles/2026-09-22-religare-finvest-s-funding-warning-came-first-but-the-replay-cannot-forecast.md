## What the lenses saw

The funding lens is a filing-period signal. It uses disclosed liability information and can operate only where a liability series exists. In the historical summary, 10 of 15 failed institutions had such disclosures. The funding signal fired first in 4 cases, with a median lead of 38 months. Those figures describe a selected historical disclosure subset. They are not a prospective hit rate and cannot speak for institutions without the relevant series.

The PCA/SAF lens is also filing-based, but it replays public-disclosure versions of the RBI’s own tripwires. Of 15 failed institutions, 5 entered an action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. It measures a different condition from a liability signal. Its apparent ordering against funding cannot establish that one condition caused the other, particularly where fraud may have impaired the reported inputs.

The model derivation is a third category of evidence. The hazard panel contains 205 rows, 9 events and 27 institutions; 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Its leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with a confidence interval of 0.338 to 1.0. A simpler heuristic reaches 0.799 on the same held-out rows. The temporal diagnostic reports 0.645 against a 0.65 gate and fails. It remains diagnostic only.

A filing-period signal is an observation placed on an availability convention. A model statistic is a derived property of a limited historical panel. Neither is a market-price signal. ## Why the warning mattered

A liability-side warning can matter because funding is a distinct balance-sheet channel. A lender must sustain assets while meeting obligations through its liability structure. Deterioration in disclosed funding conditions may therefore justify investigation before a public action-zone proxy shows a breach. That is a plausible investigative rationale, not a causal result established by this replay.

Religare Finvest fits the ordering but not the proof. Funding registered watch while the supplied PCA record has no first action-zone moment. The useful, limited implication is that a disclosed-liability deterioration can be a reason to inspect the balance sheet earlier than an action-zone lens alone would permit.

Fraud masking limits even that implication. Distorted reporting can delay, suppress or alter the measures being compared. The funding observation is not a calibrated failure probability, a determination of default risk, or a substitute for forensic work. The dossier assigns the problem of falsified filings to the forensic screen rather than claiming that disclosed thresholds can solve it.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis. This is one fraud-masked institution reconstructed under **PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT**. The historical evidence is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and lacks a bitemporal input contract. Its availability basis is an explicit publication clock when present and otherwise period end plus 60 days. Lead times are explicitly optimistic. The recorded 19-month advantage may exceed what a fully audited contemporaneous information set would have delivered.

Coverage is also selective. The funding lens only sees institutions disclosing a liability series. Five of the 15 failed institutions in the funding summary lack that observation. Missing data remain missing: they are neither evidence of sound funding nor a measured warning. A finding from the observable subset cannot automatically generalize to the unobserved subset.

The model does not rescue the story. Its temporal gate fails, while the conformal alarm is retained as a diagnostic with historical tier wiring suspended pending prospective revalidation. The strongest conclusion is therefore not that funding screens predict failure. It is that this replay makes liability disclosures a reasonable first place to investigate, while leaving open data availability, fraud masking and construction choices as explanations for the apparent order.

## The next falsifiable test

This thesis should fail if prospective, fresh, vetted public dossiers show that disclosed-liability flags do not precede independently recorded stress more often than other available lenses once an auditable publication clock is applied. It should also fail if a bitemporal dataset removes the apparent lead in fraud-masked cases or shows that the relevant filings were not available when the reconstruction assumes they were known.

Credibility would require the ordering to persist prospectively, with liability-disclosure coverage specified in advance, fraud-masked cases identified separately, missing observations retained as missing, and stale, future-dated or unclocked readings denied signal and tier authority. Until then, this is an investigative hypothesis, not investment advice.
