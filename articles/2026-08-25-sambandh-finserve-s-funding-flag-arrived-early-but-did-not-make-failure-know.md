## Why the warning mattered

The wholesale-leverage flag identifies a visible liability-side sensitivity. It does not say default was inevitable or specify when it would occur. At most, the filing supports a conditional hypothesis: wholesale-funding dependence can become a pressure point if funding conditions change before the balance sheet adjusts. The ratio is structural evidence, not proof that such a change was already under way.

That distinction matters because hindsight can turn an early ratio into an apparently complete explanation. This dossier supports only the opening link: a fragile funding classification and a stated leverage flag. It does not establish the subsequent pressure chain.

The dates belong to separate evidentiary clocks. The balance-sheet observation belongs to 2016-03-31; the public-availability proxy belongs to 2016-05-30; and the recorded event belongs to 2020-10-13. A market-price signal would require a contemporaneous price observation. A model derivation belongs to a training and validation record. Combining those categories would overstate what any lens knew at the time.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case defeats a claim that this was a timely prediction. A 52-month lead is long enough to show that the flag was not a near-term timing instrument. An institution can breach a broad funding threshold and continue operating. The historical record is labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and has no bitemporal input contract. The dossier explicitly says lead times are optimistic.

Fraud masking strengthens rather than softens that objection. If filings were falsified, no public threshold engine had a complete view of the underlying balance sheet. The lack of a Sambandh PCA finding cannot establish resilience. It also cannot prove that funding uniquely explained the outcome; it is missing comparison evidence.

A cautious reader can accept the funding structure as an early, observable vulnerability and still reject the claim that public disclosures diagnosed the full event or made the default knowable.

## The next falsifiable test

The funding thesis should fail if genuinely publication-timed future records show wholesale-funding flags do not precede independently observable funding pressure, while institutions without such flags repeatedly show pressure first. It should also fail as a timing claim if prospective cases show no useful ordering between a funding flag and later stress.

A proper test must preserve publication times, revisions, missing filings, and the fraud-masking boundary. It must assess future observations rather than fit known defaults. Until then, Sambandh remains a historical clue from a construction-PIT replay, not validated real-money evidence.
