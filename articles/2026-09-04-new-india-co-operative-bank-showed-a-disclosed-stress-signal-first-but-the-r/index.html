New India Co-operative Bank showed a disclosed stress signal first, but the replay cannot explain the whole event

The record places an NNPA action-zone breach before the later event. That is a filing-period warning, not a market-price confirmation or a model-derived failure forecast, and the fraud-masked designation sharply limits what the screen could know.

The narrow answer to LiquiLens’s question is New India Co-operative Bank. Its disclosed balance sheet entered an RBI action zone before its later event. That does not establish that a screen foresaw the failure, detected fraud, or supplied a complete causal account. The institution is marked fraud-masked. A filing can therefore contain a valid warning while remaining an incomplete, and potentially unreliable, picture of the underlying condition.

The lesson is about escalation rather than prophecy. A disclosed NNPA breach warranted investigation. It was not proof that all reported measures were accurate, a market confirmation of distress, or a calibrated probability of failure.

The record before the event

New India Co-operative Bank’s first recorded PCA/SAF action-zone signal was an NNPA breach for the period ended 2021-03-31, identified as FY2021. The replay assigns 2021-05-30 as its knowledge-time proxy. Where no explicit publication time is present, the method uses period end plus 60 days. The recorded lead from that proxy to the default date of 2025-02-13 is 44 months.

Those are distinct clocks. The period end is the accounting date of the disclosed condition. The knowledge-time proxy estimates when a reader could have encountered it. The default date marks the event used in the replay. None is a market-price timestamp, and treating them as interchangeable would exaggerate what the evidence establishes.

The case appears in the New India Co-operative Bank replay and within the Failure Radar board. Both are retrospective evidence interfaces. They do not show that an investor, depositor, supervisor, or counterparty acted on the signal at the proxy date.

Fraud masking is the central qualification. The dossier states that institutions whose filings were later shown to have been falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine. The NNPA breach remains a disclosed warning. But apparent compliance elsewhere cannot become reassurance, and the replay cannot claim access to facts not faithfully represented in public disclosure.

What the lenses saw

The PCA/SAF lens recorded a specific filing-period signal: NNPA crossed its action-zone rule in FY2021. This is the clearest finding. It says a disclosed asset-quality measure met a published tripwire. It does not say why the institution later reached its event, nor does it determine the timing of every pressure that followed.

The funding lens did not provide a first signal for this institution. That is a missing-data disclosure, not a favourable finding. The funding lens only sees institutions with a disclosed liability series, and the supplied record provides no funding signal here.

Across the PCA summary, 15 failed institutions are included, 5 entered the action zone first, and median lead was 41 months. In the funding summary, 10 of the 15 had liability disclosures, 4 had funding signals that fired first, and median lead was 38 months. These are descriptions of a replay, not prospective performance claims.

The historical evidence is labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and has no bitemporal input contract. Availability uses an explicit publication clock when present, otherwise period end plus 60 days. The dossier explicitly says lead times are optimistic. The historical validation record retains those constraints.

A separate hazard diagnostic does not rescue a stronger claim. Its leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with a 0.338 to 1.0 confidence interval. The disclosure heuristic records 0.799 on the same held-out rows. Temporal AUC is 0.645, below the 0.65 diagnostic gate. Construction-PIT diagnostics cannot promote, so this remains diagnostic only.

Why the warning mattered

The warning mattered because a disclosed action-zone condition appeared before the event. The dossier does not prove a full transmission mechanism. The defensible inference is limited: the public record supplied an asset-quality escalation point. It does not establish how much subsequent pressure travelled through earnings, capital, funding, governance, fraud, or another channel.

That restraint separates claims often collapsed into one. The NNPA breach is a filing-period signal. The supplied material contains no admitted market-price signal for this name. Scores, PDs, and tiers are model derivations governed by stated rules; they are not observations that a failure was already known to the market.

The method note says components are replayed independently and fused only for presentation. PD levels are corpus-fitted and prior-corrected to a 0.25% annual population base rate. The Merton PD is a market repricing signal, not a calibrated failure frequency. Neither construction converts the disclosed NNPA breach into proof that the model identified the later outcome in full.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case defeats a claim of prescience. A period-end proxy can place a warning earlier than the point at which a particular user could have accessed, interpreted, and trusted the relevant information. The dossier calls the resulting lead times optimistic. Without a bitemporal input contract, historical availability and amended values remain material limitations.

More fundamentally, one NNPA breach is not a complete failure narrative. The funding lens did not fire first. The bank is fraud-masked. The supplied evidence does not establish continuous deterioration from the FY2021 filing to the later event, and it does not show that a threshold can reliably separate a recoverable institution from one that will fail.

Fraud intensifies the objection. A threshold engine can identify an adverse disclosed condition while missing material facts outside reliable disclosure. The strongest conclusion is therefore not that the model caught the event. It is that the disclosed record gave a reason to investigate earlier and more deeply.

The next falsifiable test

This thesis would weaken if prospective, bitemporal evidence showed that action-zone breaches added no useful lead after actual publication availability was established, or if fraud-masked institutions made disclosed triggers indistinguishable from benign cases. It would strengthen only if eligible dossiers were frozen at authoritative publication times, later-amended values were excluded, and outcomes followed the signals.

The practical test is to preserve each eligible public dossier at its actual publication time and compare action-zone information with the disclosure heuristic prospectively. Until that exists, this remains a historical diagnostic, not a production failure forecast.
