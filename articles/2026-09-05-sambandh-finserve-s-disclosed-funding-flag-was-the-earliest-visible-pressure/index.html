Sambandh Finserve’s disclosed funding flag was the earliest visible pressure point

This historical replay identifies wholesale leverage as the first disclosed funding signal before Sambandh Finserve’s recorded default. It is not a market-price call, a causal finding, or evidence that a threshold model could see through fraud-masked filings.

1473 words · institution risk · open evidence

*This is not current news and not a forecast. It is a historical replay of Sambandh Finserve using a construction-PIT record. Similar balance-sheet mechanisms do not imply the same outcome elsewhere.*

Sambandh Finserve is the clearest answer this replay can offer to the question of which institution balance sheet should have felt financial stress first. Its funding record turned fragile at the filing period ending 2016-03-31, when disclosed wholesale leverage was 8.51x against a 7x threshold. The replay assigns that observation a knowledge-time proxy of 2016-05-30 and records a 52-month lead to the default date of 2020-10-13.

That is a filing-period signal, not a contemporaneous market-price signal and not a model-derived probability of failure. It says disclosed liabilities warranted investigation before the event. It does not establish that the ratio caused default, that creditors withdrew funding, or that the underlying disclosures were economically complete. The fraud-masked designation is decisive: a threshold engine can be reassured by filings later shown to be falsified.

The record before the event

The first scoreable funding signal is attached to the 2016-03-31 period end. Where no explicit publication clock is present, the reconstruction uses period end plus a 60-day filing lag, producing the 2016-05-30 knowledge-time proxy. That convention avoids pretending the information was known on the period-end date. It does not prove that a lender, investor, or supervisor actually possessed every relevant input then.

The funding index was 78.7, in the fragile band, with the explicit flag of wholesale leverage 8.51x >= 7x. The recorded default followed on 2020-10-13. This is the full supported sequence. The dossier does not provide a loan-book chronology, liquidity-buffer data, lender concentration, refinancing negotiations, creditor actions, or a Sambandh market-price series. It cannot support a complete causal account.

The underlying historical evidence is labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It is not validated-backtest eligible, is not real-money eligible, lacks a bitemporal input contract, and may make lead times optimistic. The Failure Radar board, historical validation record, and Sambandh replay are research materials, not proof of live decision-time knowledge.

What the lenses saw

The funding lens saw a disclosed liability-side vulnerability. Wholesale leverage crossed its stated threshold, producing the fragile classification. That makes the funding structure an investigative priority because liabilities can impose a timetable: funding may need to be refinanced, repriced, or replaced while asset cash flows may arrive later or prove less dependable. This is a mechanism implied by the category of signal, not a claim about an unobserved Sambandh refinancing event.

The PCA record did not provide a competing early warning. It has no first action-zone entry and no PCA lead time. That is an absence of a qualifying PCA result in this replay, not evidence that the institution was sound. In a fraud-masked case, apparently compliant public data are especially weak grounds for reassurance.

Across the historical failed-institution sample, the funding lens had liability disclosures for 10 of 15 failed institutions, fired first for 4, and had a median lead of 38 months. The PCA/SAF replay covered 15 failed institutions; 5 entered the action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. These are descriptive construction-PIT results. They do not validate a supervisory rule, establish an investable strategy, or turn a funding threshold into a forecast.

Why the warning mattered

A single leverage ratio cannot diagnose insolvency. Its importance is narrower: liabilities are often the balance-sheet side capable of forcing a timetable. A fragile funding classification should prompt questions about maturity, refinancing terms, cash generation, and disclosure quality. It should not settle those questions.

Sambandh’s fraud-masked status prevents the stronger conclusion. The dossier says that institutions whose filings were later shown falsified can look compliant to any threshold engine; the forensic screen owns those cases. The funding lens neither solved the case nor became irrelevant. It identified a disclosed fragility, while the record warns that disclosed fragility may understate or misstate economic reality.

This distinction also separates a filing signal from a model derivation. The funding flag is a published threshold outcome over disclosed data. The historical hazard work is a separate diagnostic exercise: its leave-one-institution-out AUC was 0.752, while the heuristic was 0.799 on the same held-out rows. Neither result changes what Sambandh’s disclosed filing did or did not reveal. The model result is not an observation of Sambandh’s market price, and it is not a calibrated claim about its failure frequency.

The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat the thesis. Wholesale leverage above 7x may be an imperfect screening convention rather than a durable measure of financial stress. A 52-month interval leaves ample room for conditions to change. The period-end-plus-60-day proxy may overstate usable lead. Most importantly, fraud masking means the public inputs may not have represented the institution’s actual condition.

The model diagnostics reinforce rather than resolve that objection. The temporal test AUC was 0.645 against a 0.65 diagnostic gate and did not pass. The conformal alarm remains diagnostic only: its historical tier wiring is suspended and it has no score or tier authority pending prospective revalidation. A skeptic can reasonably treat Sambandh as an investigative case study, not evidence of a generally deployable failure model.

The absence of a Sambandh market-price series is also material. No price-based confirmation is available in this record. Therefore the thesis cannot be that markets recognized the vulnerability first, or even that markets confirmed it at all. The replay identifies the earliest visible disclosed funding flag among the supplied lenses, not the earliest economically real stress signal.

The next falsifiable test

A prospective test would record future funding flags at actual publication time rather than a period-end proxy, then compare them with later independently observable refinancing stress, liquidity pressure, or deterioration. If flagged institutions do not show earlier or more frequent subsequent pressure than comparable unflagged institutions, the claim that this lens identifies the balance sheet that feels stress first fails.

That test must preserve negative evidence. No liability disclosure is not a clean bill of health; no market reading is not reassurance. A credible prospective record needs publication clocks, underlying disclosures, and explicit handling of fraud-masking risk before a diagnostic can be promoted. It must also keep filing observations, market observations, and model outputs separate rather than treating them as interchangeable confirmation.

Follow the pressure chain

Start with disclosed wholesale leverage and the threshold. Then ask whether funding could impose a timetable. Next test disclosure completeness and reliability, because fraud masking can sever the link between reported ratios and economic reality. Finally, separate the institution’s condition from the market’s capacity to absorb an exit.

This is a product boundary, not a combined verdict. LiquiLens research addresses institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Seiche’s overview addresses system dollar-funding capacity. Undertow’s board addresses market liquidity and executable exit capacity. They answer different questions.

The distinction matters here. A funding flag belongs to the balance-sheet inquiry. A usable market-derived reading would concern repricing or exit conditions, but the current market layer lacks publication and tier authority. A hazard statistic is a model diagnostic over its own construction record. None can repair the missing evidence in either of the others.

Sources, method, and limits

The relevant sources are the Sambandh Finserve replay, Failure Radar board, validation file, and market evidence index. Current presentation uses one row per institution with a fresh vetted public dossier; failed institutions are replayed instead. Nothing is scored from memory.
