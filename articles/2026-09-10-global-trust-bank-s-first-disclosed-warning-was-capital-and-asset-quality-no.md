*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. It examines Global Trust Bank before its default date of 2004-07-24. Similar balance-sheet mechanisms do not imply the same outcome elsewhere. This is not investment advice, and the screen is not a credit rating.*

Global Trust Bank is the narrow answer to the desk question only in one specific sense: its disclosed capital and asset-quality measures entered the first recorded RBI action zone before the event. The replay records threshold_2 breaches in CRAR and NNPA. That is a filing-period signal: reported balance-sheet weakness at a stated reporting date, made usable at a stated or proxied knowledge time. It is not a market-price signal, because no authorised market reading is used to establish this historical conclusion. And it is not a model derivation masquerading as an observed fact: the lead time follows a construction rule and carries explicit timing limits.

The thesis is therefore modest. On the public record represented here, Global Trust Bank’s first observable fault line was loss-absorption capacity exposed to impaired assets, rather than a disclosed funding alarm. It does not follow that funding was sound, that the reported ratios captured underlying reality, or that this sequence explains every part of the default.

## The record before the event

The first PCA action-zone entry is recorded at the period ending 2002-03-31, labelled FY2002. The first-action-zone record identifies CRAR and NNPA breaches, and gives 2002-05-30 as the knowledge-time proxy. The default date is 2004-07-24; the stated lead is 25 months.

Those dates answer different questions. The period end tells us when the reported condition was measured. The knowledge-time proxy estimates when an outside observer could have used it. The default date tells us when the subsequent event occurred. The replay uses an explicit publication clock when one is present and otherwise applies period end plus 60 days. That convention is a modelled availability rule, not proof of what every investor, creditor or supervisor knew on a particular day.

Global Trust Bank is fraud-masked. That disclosure is central rather than decorative: filings later shown falsified can look compliant to any threshold engine, while a reported breach may also understate or misdate real weakness. ## Why the warning mattered

CRAR and NNPA point to a plausible balance-sheet pressure chain. A capital-adequacy breach indicates less buffer against risk-weighted exposures. A net non-performing-assets breach indicates impaired assets that may require recognition, provisions or losses. If losses consume capital, a thinner buffer can reduce room for further deterioration and make confidence more fragile.

That is a mechanism, not an assertion that the ratios supplied a complete causal account. The filing-period signal says reported capital and reported asset quality crossed the relevant tripwires. It does not demonstrate a contemporaneous liability run. Nor can the absence of a scoreable funding signal establish that funding pressure arrived later.

A market-price signal would be a separately authorised reading of investor repricing. None is required for this historical finding. The current market layer is fresh in clock terms but has no admission, publication or tier authority: admitted canonical records are 0/25. It cannot be used to retrospectively validate Global Trust Bank’s warning or to reinforce a current named-institution conclusion.

## The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat any broad early-warning thesis. First, the historical evidence is PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It lacks a bitemporal input contract, is not validated-backtest eligible and is not real-money eligible. The availability proxy can make measured lead times optimistic.

Second, fraud masking attacks the premise that disclosed ratios approximate economic condition. A threshold engine cannot discover facts hidden by falsified filings. It may flag disclosed deterioration, but it cannot certify the absence of worse deterioration, establish the true timing of losses, or distinguish a reliable report from a misleading one.

Third, no scoreable funding sequence exists for Global Trust Bank. Without it, the proposed progression from impaired assets to capital compression to creditor or depositor sensitivity remains a plausible chain, not a demonstrated chain. The counter-case is not that the PCA record is irrelevant; it is that this evidence cannot prove that public ratios alone would have produced a dependable supervisory verdict, investment decision or generalisable prediction.

The correct use is an alert for investigation. It is not evidence that every CRAR-and-NNPA breach ends in default, and it is not evidence that institutions without a funding flag are safe.

## The next falsifiable test

The thesis weakens if authentic, time-stamped pre-event disclosures show that Global Trust Bank had not breached the relevant CRAR and NNPA action-zone conditions by the stated knowledge time, or show that the recorded breach derives solely from later reconstruction rather than then-available information.

It strengthens if independently time-stamped liability disclosures show subsequent funding pressure consistent with the proposed chain. Either test must preserve original publication timing and identify falsified filings. Otherwise, apparent lead time remains potentially optimistic.

## Follow the pressure chain

Start with impaired assets and reported loss recognition. Ask whether those pressures compress capital. Then ask whether a reduced buffer affects confidence and whether liability pressure is independently observable. Do not reverse the sequence merely because funding distress is easier to narrate after a failure.

For current work, require fresh vetted public dossiers, remove stale, future-dated and unclocked readings from signal or tier authority, and treat absent liability disclosure as unknown. **Institutions without vetted dossiers are absent by design: nothing here is scored from memory.**
