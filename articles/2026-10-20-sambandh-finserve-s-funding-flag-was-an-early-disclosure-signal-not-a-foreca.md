## Why the warning mattered

Wholesale leverage matters because it raises a balance-sheet question about refinancing dependence. If asset performance weakens, earnings and capital capacity may be less able to absorb losses. Where wholesale liabilities are important, funding terms and rollover willingness may become more consequential. That is a plausible pressure chain, not an established account of what happened at Sambandh.

The flag therefore mattered as a prompt for harder investigation of funding concentration, maturity structure, liquidity resilience, lender diversity, and loss-absorption capacity. The fragile label does not demonstrate that a lender withdrew, repriced, shortened, or refused funding. The dossier supplies a threshold flag, not lender-by-lender rollover evidence, cash-flow history, or a verified transmission sequence.

This distinction is essential. A filing signal can reveal a disclosed structural exposure. A market-price signal would require authorized evidence of repricing or drawdown. Neither establishes causation. A model derivation would require an eligible prospective framework able to translate inputs into a validated probability. This record explicitly does not meet that standard.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat an overconfident thesis. Wholesale funding is not inherently fatal. An institution can carry substantial wholesale liabilities while asset quality, capital capacity, liquidity buffers, maturities, funding diversity, and lender relationships remain resilient. A ratio threshold is a coarse representation of a more complex balance sheet.

The historical model evidence is not decisive either. The hazard panel contains 205 rows, 9 events, and 27 institutions, while 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Its leave-one-institution-out AUC is 0.752, with a 95% interval from 0.338 to 1.0. The heuristic comparator reports 0.799 on the same held-out rows. The temporal diagnostic AUC is 0.645, below the 0.65 gate, and remains diagnostic only.

A sceptic can reasonably conclude that the flag marked a broad vulnerability rather than a dependable countdown. Fraud masking intensifies the objection: incomplete or false disclosures can make the relationship between reported leverage and actual condition unknowable. The defensible conclusion is limited to this: Sambandh’s disclosed funding profile justified more investigation than a comparable profile without the flag.

## The next falsifiable test

The thesis weakens if prospectively timestamped, vetted disclosures show flagged institutions retaining refinancing access, liquidity resilience, and loss-absorption capacity through stress while comparable unflagged institutions deteriorate first. It strengthens only if original filing vintages, explicit publication clocks, liability composition, and later outcomes are preserved before results are known.

That test cannot be satisfied by extending a retrospective replay. It requires a pre-specified prospective cohort and evaluation that does not inherit revised knowledge from later filings or outcomes.
