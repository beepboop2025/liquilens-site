*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. Madhavpura Mercantile Co-operative Bank defaulted on 2012-06-04. Similar mechanisms do not imply the same outcome at any institution today.*

The conclusion from this replay is narrow. The institution that should feel stress first is not always the institution that a public-data balance-sheet engine can identify first. Madhavpura is fraud-masked. Its funding lens was not scoreable, and the replay records no first action-zone entry. Those absences do not establish calm before the event. They establish that the disclosed record did not yield a conventional warning through these lenses.

A filing-period signal, a market-price signal, and a model derivation are different objects. A filing-period signal describes what a dated public disclosure permits a screen to observe. A market-price signal is a repricing observation only when its inputs are authorized for that use. A model derivation turns specified inputs into a score or tier; it cannot repair falsified or missing inputs. Madhavpura is a test of that boundary, not proof that hidden stress was visible in advance.

## What the lenses saw

The PCA lens replays public-disclosure versions of RBI action-zone tripwires. Among failed institutions in its summary, 5 of 15 entered the action zone first, with median lead of 41 months. The funding lens is narrower: it can operate only where an institution discloses a liability series. Of 15 failed institutions, 10 had those disclosures; 4 produced a funding signal first, with median lead of 38 months.

Madhavpura is not evidence for either summary result. Its funding evidence was not scoreable and its PCA replay records no first action-zone entry. The dossier’s limitation is direct: filings later shown to be falsified can appear compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns that problem.

That is not a semantic distinction. A conventional balance-sheet model begins with reported assets, liabilities, capital, and funding behaviour. If reports misrepresent the relevant condition, a model can calculate faithfully from filings while describing the institution poorly. A benign-looking ratio would then be a property of the reported input, not a verified property of the balance sheet.

The broader India diagnostic covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. But it is labelled a filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT diagnostic, not validated backtest evidence. Madhavpura sits at the fraud-masked boundary rather than within the non-fraud headline result. ## Why the warning mattered

The warning is methodological: fraud masking must remain a forensic limitation, not become an unflagged balance-sheet verdict. Calling an institution safe because a disclosed threshold did not fire would confuse an information failure with a credit conclusion.

The wider validation does not erase this constraint. Its hazard panel has 205 rows, 9 events, and 27 institutions, with 179 censored or unusable rows excluded. Leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with a confidence interval of 0.338 to 1.0; the comparable heuristic AUC is 0.799. The temporal diagnostic AUC is 0.645, below its 0.65 gate. That diagnostic cannot promote because construction-PIT evidence cannot promote.

These are diagnostic results, not a claim that a warning was investable or available in real time. Historical evidence uses an explicit publication clock when present and otherwise a period-end plus 60-days proxy. It lacks a bitemporal input contract, is not validated-backtest eligible, is not real-money eligible, and its lead times are optimistic.

## The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case defeats an overextended thesis. The replay is too sparse to establish that Madhavpura’s balance sheet visibly deteriorated in a particular sequence before default. Neither conventional lens records a first signal, and the dossier supplies no line-item chronology from which to reconstruct one. “Unknown” is more defensible than a claim that hidden stress was demonstrably present at a specific earlier point.

Fraud masking explains why silence cannot be reassurance; it does not prove that a public balance-sheet screen could have identified fraud earlier. The counter-case should prevail unless independently auditable forensic evidence supplies that missing bridge.

Institutions without vetted dossiers are absent by design; nothing is scored from memory. The conformal alarm is diagnostic only, with historical tier wiring suspended and no score or tier authority pending prospective revalidation. Those rules stop a plausible narrative from becoming unsupported evidence.

## The next falsifiable test

The thesis would weaken if a prospectively assembled, genuinely bitemporal record of fraud-masked cases showed that disclosed PCA, funding, or other public balance-sheet measures repeatedly gave timely, independently verified warnings before forensic evidence emerged. It would strengthen if such cases repeatedly appeared compliant on published thresholds while later evidence showed that reported inputs masked the relevant risk.

The test must preserve actual filing availability rather than substitute a period-end proxy where publication time is unknown. It must distinguish missing liability disclosure from stable funding, assess forensic evidence separately from threshold performance, and refuse to treat a model derivation as either a market-price signal or proof of a reported balance sheet.

## Follow the pressure chain

For a normally disclosed institution, the pressure chain runs from reported balance-sheet condition to capital or action-zone pressure, then to liabilities and disclosed funding behaviour, and only then to authorized market repricing. Every link requires a dated, vetted input. A disclosure break is a limitation, not evidence that pressure stopped.

For a fraud-masked institution, the prior question is whether the public record can be trusted to represent assets, liabilities, and capital. If that remains unresolved, ratios may accurately summarize filings while failing to summarize the institution. That is the usable lesson from Madhavpura, and no broader one is warranted.
