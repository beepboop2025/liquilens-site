Madhavpura Mercantile Co-operative Bank presents the hardest version of LiquiLens’s desk question: which institution balance sheet should feel financial stress first, and why? The honest answer cannot be manufactured from an incomplete record. The dossier classifies Madhavpura as fraud-masked, records no PCA first action-zone date or lead-month measure, and says the funding lens is not scoreable.

That does not mean the institution was sound before its recorded default. Nor does it show that a public disclosure model detected the problem late. It means the supplied evidence does not support either claim. A filing-period signal describes what an eligible disclosed record produced. A market-price signal describes repricing in a market series. A model derivation combines admitted inputs under stated rules. None can repair a compromised or unavailable institution-level disclosure record.

Madhavpura is classified as an urban co-operative bank, with a recorded default date of 2012-06-04. The dossier marks it fraud-masked. No institution-specific filing as-of date is supplied. That absence matters: a missing publication or filing date cannot be converted into proof that the reported position was stable, timely, or representative of the economic position.

The framework’s method note identifies the central problem directly: an institution whose filings were later shown to have been falsified can appear compliant to a threshold engine. In that circumstance, the apparent filing result concerns the disclosed record, not necessarily the underlying balance sheet. The forensic screen, rather than a ratio threshold, owns that limitation.

## What the lenses saw

The PCA lens supplies no usable first-warning sequence for this institution. It is a filing-period lens: its relevant observation is whether disclosed and eligible inputs meet an action-zone rule. For Madhavpura, the dossier does not report the first action-zone date or a lead-month measure. There is therefore no institution-specific PCA timing claim to make.

The funding lens is even more limited. It is not scoreable because the required liability disclosure series was unavailable to that lens. An unscoreable funding result is not evidence of secure funding. It says that the model’s required input was not available in a form that made the institution eligible for the calculation.

Neither result is a market-price signal. The record supplies no admitted Madhavpura market-price measure, drawdown, or market-derived tier. A market price, where properly authorized, could show investor repricing; it would not by itself verify the balance-sheet condition. Here, it is absent as institution-specific evidence.

The wider historical summaries are useful context but cannot be used to fill Madhavpura’s missing fields. ## Why the warning mattered

The warning in this replay is methodological rather than predictive. A reported capital, asset-quality, liquidity, or funding measure is an observation from a filing-period record. It is not automatically a verified statement about the economic balance sheet. Where the disclosed record is compromised, a threshold result can be accurately calculated and still fail to describe the underlying condition.

A model derivation has a separate boundary. It may fuse eligible components for presentation, but it cannot infer reassurance from unavailable inputs. It cannot transform an unscoreable funding series into a calm funding finding, and it cannot interpret an unreported PCA event date as evidence that no stress existed.

That is why fraud masking must remain visible. Folding such cases into a simple success or failure count would exaggerate what the screen observed. Madhavpura is not evidence that the PCA lens warned first, that funding warned first, or that either lens had a measured lead. It is evidence that disclosure integrity comes before any model claim.

## The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat an overbroad thesis: disclosure screens should not be expected to reveal information that was concealed, unavailable, or not eligible for their rules. Their performance should be judged on the disclosed records they can actually observe.

There is supportive, but bounded, evidence for usefulness in observable cases. The India evidence index covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. Yet the same evidence is identified as a filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT diagnostic. It is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and does not have a bitemporal input contract.

Where an explicit publication clock is unavailable, the construction uses period end plus 60 days. The dossier explicitly says lead times are optimistic. That qualification can defeat any claim that the reported lead is equivalent to a tradable or contemporaneously knowable warning. The PCA and funding summaries therefore demonstrate conditional historical signal behavior, not a clean real-time record and not a Madhavpura-specific detection.

The counter-case is stronger than a disclaimer: public disclosures may be useful when disclosures are credible and eligible, while being unable to penetrate a fraud-masked record. Madhavpura does not disprove the lenses. It does disprove the claim that their non-signal was reassuring.

## The next falsifiable test

This interpretation would weaken if preserved pre-default disclosures established that Madhavpura crossed the PCA-style action zone before 2012-06-04. It would also weaken if a qualifying liability series produced an earlier funding signal under the stated rule. Either result would create a measured institution-specific lead that the current dossier does not provide.

The opposite result would also be informative. If the historical record continued to contain no usable eligible disclosures for either lens, fraud masking and missing eligibility would remain the better explanation for the non-signal. That would not establish that every fraud-masked institution behaves alike. It would preserve the narrower claim about this case and this record.

## Follow the pressure chain

The required sequence is disclosure integrity, lens eligibility, filing-period signal, model derivation, authority, and only then an institution-risk conclusion. Madhavpura breaks that chain at disclosure integrity and funding eligibility. A non-reported PCA timing field and an unscoreable funding field cannot be recast as a calm balance sheet.
