First Leasing replay: when a public balance-sheet radar cannot see through the record

First Leasing Company Of India’s fraud-masked default is not an early-warning success. It is a boundary case showing why a filing-period signal, a market-price signal and a model derivation must not be treated as the same evidence.

1440 words · institution risk · open evidence

*Historical replay — not current news and not a forecast.* First Leasing Company Of India defaulted on 2013-12-02. The useful lesson is not that a public screen called the event. The supplied record does not establish that it did. Rather, it shows a harder limitation: an institution can be under severe economic pressure while its reported balance sheet fails to transmit that pressure to a threshold-based public-data system.

The distinction matters for the desk question: which balance sheet should feel stress first, and why? A filing-period signal describes what disclosed reports showed at an available reporting point. A market-price signal describes repricing in tradable instruments, when the market data are authorized for that use. A model derivation combines permitted inputs under a stated rule. None can be silently substituted for another; none proves the underlying economic condition by itself.

First Leasing is marked fraud_masked in this replay. Its case therefore tests the boundary of disclosed-data surveillance, rather than supplying evidence that an ordinary balance-sheet deterioration was identified in time.

The record before the event

First Leasing Company Of India is classified as an NBFC, with default date 2013-12-02. Its PCA record contains no first action-zone date and no lead-month figure. Its funding record is scoreable, but contains no first signal and no lead-month figure. These are missing replay results, not affirmative findings that funding was calm, capital was adequate or the institution was safe.

The historical framework explicitly warns that institutions whose filings were later shown falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns those cases. That warning is central here. The dossier does not provide underlying accounting particulars that would establish the route from misstatement to default. It does establish that the reported public record did not produce an earlier PCA action-zone entry or funding signal in this replay.

The India evidence is broader but tightly qualified. It covers 48 institutions across two decades, with a headline finding that 88.9% of non-fraud failures were flagged and median lead was 21.5 months. Yet its status is PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT: it is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible and has no bitemporal input contract. Availability uses an explicit publication clock when present; otherwise it uses period end plus 60 days. The dossier says lead times are optimistic. These are diagnostic construction results, not tradable performance evidence. See the Failure Radar board, validation record and First Leasing replay.

What the lenses saw

The PCA lens is a public-disclosure replay of the RBI’s PCA/SAF tripwires. Among 15 failed institutions, 5 entered an action zone first, with median lead of 41 months. That is evidence that disclosed supervisory-style thresholds can be informative in some cases. It is not evidence that they identified First Leasing: its supplied PCA fields show no action-zone date and no lead time.

The funding lens is narrower because it requires a disclosed liability series. Of 15 failed institutions, 10 had liability disclosures; the funding signal fired first for 4, with median lead of 38 months. First Leasing is marked scoreable, yet no first funding signal is recorded. That is a non-result in the replay, not a clean bill of health.

A filing-period signal must also be separated from a model derivation. The published rule can fuse disclosed levels, deterioration, a funding flag, a forensic indicator and an authoritative market drawdown into a presentation tier. PD levels are corpus-fitted and prior-corrected to a ~0.25%/yr population base rate using King-Zeng. That makes them model outputs, not direct observations. The conformal alarm is retained only as a diagnostic: its historical tier wiring is suspended and it has no score or tier authority pending prospective revalidation.

A market-price signal is different again. The dossier describes Merton PD as a market repricing signal, not a calibrated failure frequency. For the current market layer, data are fresh and clocked, but display, model-gate and tier use are not approved; admitted canonical records are 0/25. Market information therefore cannot authorize a current tier in this record.

Why the warning mattered

A reported balance sheet is supposed to connect asset quality, loss absorption and creditor confidence. If losses, impairment or other relevant conditions are not truthfully represented, reported capital and risk measures can remain clear of a threshold. If disclosure does not reveal why liability providers should reassess the institution, a funding lens may not fire either.

That is an inference about the limits of observable inputs, not a reconstructed account of First Leasing’s finances. The dossier supports the narrower conclusion: in a fraud-masked case, apparent compliance in disclosed data may be an artefact of the record rather than evidence of resilience. A missed signal in an honest, timely disclosure regime could challenge the threshold. A missed signal where filings were later shown falsified can instead show that the measured balance sheet was not a reliable map of the institution.

The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat any inflated version of this thesis. Perhaps the framework is simply too weak to identify stress reliably. First Leasing has no recorded PCA or funding lead. Historical availability is sometimes proxied using period end plus 60 days, and the dossier expressly calls the lead times optimistic.

The hazard evidence is also limited. The panel has 205 rows, 9 events and 27 institutions; 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Leave-one-institution-out AUC is 0.752, with an interval of 0.338 to 1.0. On the same held-out rows, the heuristic AUC is 0.799, above the hazard AUC. The temporal test has AUC of 0.645, below the 0.65 diagnostic gate, and cannot promote construction-PIT diagnostics.

That critique means this replay cannot demonstrate that the radar would have protected a creditor, investor or supervisor. Nor can a fraud_masked label solve forensic identification after the fact. The defensible claim is smaller: the system reports the absence and limits of disclosed signals rather than converting unavailable or compromised evidence into reassurance.

The next falsifiable test

The thesis weakens if a genuinely bitemporal, prospectively audited sample finds that disclosed PCA, funding and forensic signals do not identify pressure before adverse outcomes better than chance. It also weakens if exact publication clocks erase the diagnostic lead, or if fraud-masked cases cannot be distinguished through a documented process independent of the outcome.

It gains credibility only if prospective records preserve what was knowable when, retain missing and stale observations instead of filling them with calm, and separately test filing signals, market-price signals and model derivations. This is not investment advice and not a credit rating.

Follow the pressure chain

The disciplined sequence is reported assets and capital; disclosed liability structure; supervisory-style thresholds; forensic indicators; then market and exit conditions where authority permits. A break should prompt investigation, not automatic certainty.

LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. Undertow covers market liquidity and executable exit capacity. Those are product boundaries, not mutual confirmation. The handoff is diagnostic: institution records locate disclosed balance-sheet pressure; system and exit tools ask whether funding conditions or execution conditions corroborate it.
