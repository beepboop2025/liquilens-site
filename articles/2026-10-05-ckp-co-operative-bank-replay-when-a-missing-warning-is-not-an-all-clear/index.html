CKP Co-operative Bank replay: when a missing warning is not an all-clear

CKP Co-operative Bank’s historical file does not establish a clean advance balance-sheet alarm before default. It instead shows why an unscoreable institution must remain an observability problem, not be recast as a calm one.

1310 words · institution risk · open evidence

*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. CKP Co-operative Bank defaulted on 2020-04-30. The Failure Radar snapshot is as of 2026-10-05. The market pack is as of 2026-10-01 and was retrieved on 2026-10-05. Those current reference dates do not change the historical nature of the CKP record.*

CKP Co-operative Bank is a hard test of the proposition that a balance-sheet screen can identify which institution should feel financial stress first. The dossier does not show that a PCA-style threshold or funding signal supplied a clean, measurable advance warning before its default. It also does not support the opposite conclusion: that the absence of such a signal was evidence of resilience.

That distinction is more than a caveat. A filing-period signal says what can be reconstructed from a reported balance-sheet record under a stated availability convention. A market-price signal is a separate repricing observation, and this dossier does not authorize market-derived display, model, or tier use. A model derivation is a rule applied to the inputs; it is not an independently observed institutional fact. CKP’s central result is therefore non-observability, rather than a demonstrated clean bill of health or a demonstrated early alarm.

The record before the event

CKP Co-operative Bank is classified as a UCB and has a default date of 2020-04-30. Its PCA record contains no first action zone and no measured lead months. Its funding lens is not scoreable, with no first signal and no measured lead months. The file is not fraud-masked.

Those are deliberately narrow statements. They do not document a PCA action-zone breach that can be presented as the first public warning. They do not document a funding signal. Nor do they establish that the underlying balance sheet was sound. The dedicated CKP replay and Failure Radar board are constrained reconstructions, not contemporaneous credit opinions.

The historical evidence status is PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It is not eligible as a validated backtest, is not eligible for real-money use, and lacks a bitemporal input contract. Where an explicit publication clock exists, it is used; otherwise availability is set at period end plus 60 days. The dossier explicitly says lead times may be optimistic.

That is a research boundary, not a footnote. A filing-period reconstruction cannot prove that an outside observer possessed, trusted, and could interpret an input on a particular historical date when its availability is proxied. The missing warning is consequently not proof that the warning mechanism failed, nor proof that CKP was calm before the event.

What the lenses saw

The PCA lens replays public-disclosure versions of the RBI’s PCA/SAF tripwires. In the historical summary, 5 of 15 failed institutions entered an action zone first, with median lead of 41 months. That aggregate result cannot be assigned to CKP: its record has no first action-zone entry.

The funding lens has an even stricter dependency. Of 15 failed institutions, 10 had liability disclosures; a funding signal fired first for 4, with median lead of 38 months. The lens can only see institutions that disclose a liability series. CKP is not scoreable under it. “No funding signal” here means no authorized funding-lens conclusion, not no funding risk.

The broader India diagnostic covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. But it carries the same filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT status and optimistic-lead-time warning. The validation record shows a temporal AUC of 0.645, below the 0.65 diagnostic gate. Its construction-PIT diagnostics cannot promote.

Why the warning mattered

A balance-sheet mechanism is observable only after relevant information is disclosed, mapped, and admitted to the rule. Asset-quality damage, losses, capital erosion, or funding strain may precede that process. If a liability series is unavailable, the funding channel cannot be scored. If a publication clock is inferred, the apparent lead may overstate what was knowable. If a vetted dossier is absent, the architecture does not score from memory.

CKP therefore identifies an institution-risk boundary: rankable risk and unobservable risk must not be merged into the same reassuring category. LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. In this case, the institution-level evidence does not establish an authorized pre-default ranking. That is not a reason to invent a score; it is a reason not to translate non-scoreability into green.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat a careless version of this thesis. A disciplined screen should not manufacture distress from missing information. CKP has no fraud-masked flag. The dossier reports neither a PCA action-zone entry nor a funding trigger nor measured lead months. Automatically escalating every incomplete file would confuse missingness with demonstrated weakness and could create false positives.

That argument is strong. The method says institutions without vetted dossiers are absent by design, and nothing is scored from memory. The conformal alarm is diagnostic only; its historical tier wiring is suspended and it has no score or tier authority pending prospective revalidation. Those controls prevent a retrospective story from becoming a fabricated signal.

But the counter-case fails if refusal to score is recast as reassurance. “Not scoreable” is a data-state conclusion. It is neither green nor a prediction of failure. CKP supports that limited, but important, conclusion.

The next falsifiable test

The thesis is falsifiable. A prospective record would need time-stamped, vetted disclosures for comparable institutions, including liability series where funding analysis is intended. It should then test whether authorized PCA or funding signals precede severe stress using actual publication-time availability rather than a period-end proxy.

If non-scoreable institutions prove no more vulnerable than fully observable ones, the claim that non-scoreability deserves separate escalation weakens. If complete contemporaneous disclosures repeatedly show timely clean all-clear signals before stress, the thesis weakens further. Conversely, repeated stress in the non-scoreable cohort would support an observability escalation category, still without turning it into a failure forecast.
