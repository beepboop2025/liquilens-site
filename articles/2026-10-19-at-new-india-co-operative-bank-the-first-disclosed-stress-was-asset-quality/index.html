At New India Co-operative Bank, the first disclosed stress was asset quality, not funding

The replay places an NNPA action-zone breach at the FY2021 filing period, ahead of the 2025-02-13 event date. That is a filing-period signal under a construction-PIT proxy, not a market-price warning, a validated forecast, or proof that the record revealed fraud.

1382 words · institution risk · open evidence

*This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. It examines New India Co-operative Bank around its 2025-02-13 event date. Similar mechanisms do not imply the same outcome elsewhere.*

The narrow answer to the desk question is New India Co-operative Bank: disclosed balance-sheet stress appeared first in the asset-quality lens. The replay records an RBI action-zone breach for NNPA in FY2021, for the period ending 2021-03-31. Its assigned knowledge-time proxy is 2021-05-30, and the recorded lead is 44 months.

That finding requires careful classification. It is a filing-period signal: a breach derived from a reported period, with availability assigned by an explicit publication clock when available or by period end plus 60 days otherwise. It is not a market-price signal, and it is not a model-derived probability of failure. The record is fraud-masked and labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT; it is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and has no bitemporal input contract. The dossier says its lead times are optimistic.

The record before the event

The first action-zone observation was FY2021. The listed breach was NNPA. The replay links the 2021-05-30 knowledge-time proxy to the 2021-03-31 period end, before the 2025-02-13 event. This supports a limited sequencing claim: the disclosed asset-quality screen fired before the recorded event.

The funding lens did not provide an earlier result. New India’s funding record is scoreable, but no first funding signal or funding lead is reported. This is missing signal evidence, not affirmative evidence of calm funding. The funding lens can assess only institutions that disclose a liability series. The absence of an earlier disclosed funding flag does not establish that deposits, liquidity, or other liabilities were free from pressure.

The fraud-masked designation is material. A threshold engine sees reported inputs rather than concealed conditions. The dossier notes that filings later shown falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine, with the forensic screen owning that limitation. A public-disclosure replay cannot convert a reported ratio into proof of institutional condition, solvency, or safety.

What the lenses saw

The regulatory lens observed an NNPA breach. The funding lens did not establish an earlier disclosed signal. That is the complete supported ordering. It permits the conclusion that asset-quality deterioration was the earliest visible warning in this replay. It does not support the stronger claim that funding was sound, irrelevant, or later in the institution’s underlying condition.

Across the historical summaries, lenses do not have a universal order. Of 15 failed institutions in the PCA/SAF summary, 5 entered the action zone first, with median lead of 41 months. Of those 15, 10 had liability disclosures in the funding summary; funding fired first for 4, with median lead of 38 months. These are descriptive public-disclosure replays, not an ordering rule for all lenders.

The PCA/SAF exercise replays RBI tripwires using public disclosures. Timing begins at an exact publication time where available and otherwise uses the period-end-plus-60-days proxy. The Failure Radar board and historical validation record should be read before treating any lead-time statistic as investable evidence.

Why the warning mattered

NNPA is a filing-period indicator of disclosed asset-quality stress. The warning mattered because it raised a balance-sheet question before the event: whether reported problem assets were eroding the institution’s capacity to absorb further deterioration. That is an investigative question, not a demonstrated causal history.

Any fuller chain is a model derivation rather than an observed fact in this dossier. Impaired-asset pressure may require greater loss absorption; reduced capacity may make confidence or funding pressure more consequential. But the supplied evidence contains no series for capital, provisions, depositor behaviour, cash flows, or a causal explanation for the event. It supports an earlier NNPA screen, not a complete postmortem.

The New India replay and the wider replay archive are therefore best used as historical investigation tools, not as current institution dossiers.

The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat the thesis: the reported NNPA ratio may not have reliably represented the balance sheet. The fraud-masked label makes that possibility central. If disclosures were incomplete or distorted, an apparently early ratio breach may not describe the true onset, scale, or mechanism of stress.

Timing is a further objection. The 44-month lead uses a knowledge-time proxy, not a fully verified vintage archive. The period-end-plus-60-days convention is more conservative than dating information to the reporting period, but remains a construction assumption where no exact publication clock exists. It is consequently an optimistic historical lead, not a tradable timestamp.

Model diagnostics do not rescue the claim. The temporal hazard test reported AUC of 0.645 against a diagnostic gate of 0.65 and did not pass. Historical tier wiring for the conformal alarm is suspended pending prospective revalidation; it has no score or tier authority. On the same held-out rows, the heuristic AUC was 0.799 against hazard AUC of 0.752. None validates this case as a forecast.

The thesis also weakens if complete, timely liability data repeatedly provides earlier verified funding warnings in comparable institutions. A missing funding signal is not a clean liquidity bill of health.

The next falsifiable test

A proper test would preserve actual publication timestamps, retain missing-data states, isolate forensic exceptions, and assess a prospective cohort only after its outcome window closes. The thesis fails if vintage-controlled evidence shows that NNPA action-zone breaches do not precede later balance-sheet stress more often than competing lenses after fraud masking and liability-disclosure gaps are handled.

It also fails if a complete liability dataset repeatedly supplies earlier verified funding signals for comparable institutions. Until then, 44 months is an investigative replay result—not a calibrated probability, a failure forecast, or evidence for a trading decision.

Follow the pressure chain

The disciplined chain is disclosed NNPA pressure, evidence of capacity to absorb further loss, then independently evidenced funding strain. Each link needs its own record. A missing funding signal is not reassuring, and system context cannot fill an institution-level disclosure gap.
