Nedungadi Bank’s capital breach was the first visible warning in the disclosed record

A historical replay places Nedungadi Bank’s first RBI capital action-zone breach before default. That is a bounded filing-period signal, not a market-price signal, a live forecast, or proof that capital caused the failure.

1442 words · institution risk · open evidence

This historical replay is not current news and not a forecast. It uses a construction-PIT record that is not eligible for validated-backtest or real-money use. Similar mechanisms do not imply the same outcome.

Nedungadi Bank offers a narrow answer to LiquiLens’s question: which balance sheet should appear stressed first, and why? In the available record, the first observable warning was regulatory capital. CRAR crossed the first RBI action-zone threshold in the period ending 1999-03-31, identified as FY1999. The replay assigns a knowledge-time proxy of 1999-05-30. Default followed on 2002-11-02, with a stated lead of 41 months.

That chronology supports a limited proposition only. Capital was the first visible pressure point among the lenses eligible in this replay. It does not establish that capital caused the default, that funding was sound, or that public observers had a complete contemporaneous record. A filing-period signal reports what a disclosed balance-sheet measure showed at a reporting date. A market-price signal is a market repricing observation. A model derivation is an output produced by data, assumptions, and rules. None can be substituted for another.

The record before the event

The historical record puts Nedungadi Bank into its first action-zone status at the first threshold and identifies CRAR as the breach. The institution is not marked fraud-masked. That makes the capital observation usable within this replay; it does not make the disclosed record complete.

The funding lens is explicitly unscoreable. It records no first signal and no funding lead time. This is a missing-data and eligibility disclosure, not evidence of stable liabilities. The funding lens needs a liability series, and the record does not provide the scoreable disclosure necessary for Nedungadi Bank. An unavailable funding result cannot be recast as evidence that funding pressure was absent.

Timing is conditional as well. The reporting period ended on 1999-03-31. Where no explicit publication clock is present, the replay uses period end plus 60 days, producing 1999-05-30 here. The dossier says lead times are optimistic and that the record lacks a bitemporal input contract. The stated 41 months is therefore a construction-PIT chronology, not proof of the precise moment at which an investor, depositor, creditor, or supervisor could have acted.

Read the Nedungadi Bank replay alongside the Failure Radar board and the historical validation record. They document a period-end-proxy construction-PIT exercise: a means of examining disclosed warning sequences, not a validated backtest or a real-money tool.

What the lenses saw

The PCA lens saw a CRAR breach. The funding lens did not produce an eligible result. The first is a filing-period threshold signal; the second is a data-availability status. Neither is a market-price observation.

The wider PCA summary covers 15 failed institutions. 5 entered an action zone first, with a median lead of 41 months. It is a public-disclosure replay of RBI PCA and SAF tripwires. The funding summary covers a narrower eligible set: 10 of 15 failed institutions had liability disclosures, 4 had a funding signal fire first, and the median lead was 38 months. The denominators matter. The lenses do not observe the same universe, so their apparent ordering is not a clean contest between capital and funding.

The dossier also says institutions whose filings were later shown falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns that limitation. Nedungadi Bank is not marked fraud-masked, but the wider constraint remains: a disclosed ratio cannot demonstrate that every underlying disclosure was complete or accurate.

Model evidence needs a third, separate reading. The hazard panel contains 205 rows, 9 events, and 27 institutions; 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Its leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with a confidence interval from 0.338 to 1.0. The disclosure heuristic is 0.799 on the same held-out rows. The temporal diagnostic AUC is 0.645, below the 0.65 gate, and is diagnostic only. These are model derivations from a limited construction record, not observed default probabilities for Nedungadi Bank.

Why the warning mattered

The warning mattered because an RBI action-zone breach identifies a disclosed regulatory-capital constraint. That is more precise than saying the bank was destined to fail. It directs further investigation toward the capital buffer, the reporting that supports it, and whether subsequent evidence confirms or contradicts pressure elsewhere on the balance sheet.

The interval before default means the breach was not merely a final datapoint in this replay. But a long measured lead does not become a tradable rule, a causal explanation, or a forecast. Historical lead times are explicitly described as optimistic. Because the funding series was not scoreable, this case cannot show that capital beat funding for Nedungadi Bank rather than being the only eligible early lens.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis. A capital threshold may be a lagging, coarse regulatory disclosure rather than the first economically meaningful indication of strain. Risks may exist before a period-end ratio captures them. A capital ratio does not resolve funding durability, asset liquidity, external support, or information unavailable in public filings.

More fundamentally, capital may seem first because competing lenses could not score the institution. The missing funding result prevents this record from establishing that capital was uniquely earlier than funding. The period-end-plus-60-day clock may also make the apparent warning interval look more actionable than it was. Without a bitemporal input contract, that is a material limitation rather than a technical footnote.

The proposition should fail if prospectively timestamped, eligibility-matched evidence shows that action-zone breaches add no useful information beyond funding and forensic signals. It should also fail if actual publication clocks erase the apparent lead. This historical sequence cannot settle either question.

The next falsifiable test

A credible test would use vetted public dossiers, explicit publication clocks, and matched eligibility across capital, funding, and forensic lenses. It would preserve absent liability-series disclosures as missing rather than treating them as calm. It would separate fraud-masked institutions and test whether an action-zone breach adds information after the other eligible lenses are known.

The Nedungadi proposition fails if capital does not lead subsequent deterioration more usefully than eligible alternatives, or if measured lead disappears when proxy clocks are replaced by observed publication times. Until then, construction-PIT diagnostics cannot be promoted into a live failure forecast.

Follow the pressure chain

The supported chain is deliberately short. A disclosed capital-threshold breach identifies where balance-sheet investigation should start; other evidence must then confirm, weaken, or contradict the case. Nedungadi Bank supports the middle observation: CRAR breached an action-zone threshold before default. It does not establish every upstream cause or downstream transmission.

LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. Undertow covers market liquidity and executable exit capacity. These are distinct diagnostics. Their outputs should not be fused into one assertion of stress.
