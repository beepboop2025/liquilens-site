## The finding

Utkarsh Small Finance Bank is the balance sheet in this monitored set most likely to register visible financial pressure first. That is a conditional filing-based finding, not a claim that failure is imminent. The current orange tier rests on disclosed asset quality and a modelled monitoring probability, while the evidence needed to establish a funding event or a market repricing is unavailable or inadmissible.

The key disclosed burden is GNPA of 9.4%. The subject dossier is as of 2025-03-31 and is aged 18 months at publication. Its monitored PD_12m is 0.0031, compared with 0.0016 at the FY2024 reference point; delta_pd_12m is 0.0015. Those are model derivations from the filing record, not observed market prices and not a credit rating. The model basis explicitly identifies GNPA 9.4%.

That distinction is central. A filing-period signal says what the reported balance sheet showed at a stated reporting date. A market-price signal would say how tradable claims were priced at an admissible observation time. A model derivation transforms disclosed inputs into a monitoring estimate under stated assumptions. None of those is interchangeable. The orange tier is a prioritisation device for investigation, not an investment recommendation or an assertion of default.

## The mechanism

The proposed mechanism begins with impaired assets. A high GNPA ratio can become an earnings and capital problem if provisioning, recoveries, and operating earnings do not offset the credit burden. Capital then has less capacity to support lending, absorb later shocks, or reassure funding providers. The pressure chain is plausible, but the dossier does not supply a future loss path, recovery path, or provisioning trajectory. It therefore supports a vulnerability thesis, not a completed causal diagnosis.

Reported CRAR was 20.93, with 5.93 percentage points of headroom over the 15.0 licensing CRAR floor used in this assessment. That is meaningful reported capacity. It is not evidence that capital cannot erode, nor does it identify the speed at which losses might consume the buffer. Utkarsh is outside the 2021 bank-PCA framework, so the dossier does not allege a PCA breach. Treating licensing-floor headroom as a PCA all-clear would be a category error.

The narrower call is that credit-cost and capital pressure are the first visible route because they are the route the filing discloses. It does not say funding is safe. Funding is not scored, and the relevant lenses are dark.

## The evidence that is dark

The deposit-run lens is dark because there are no consecutive deposit disclosures. Wholesale reliance, CD strain, and LCR headroom are also dark. Those gaps block a full assessment of funding pressure. They do not demonstrate an absence of funding risk.

Forensics are ineligible. There is no forensic verdict and no forensic indicator. That is a missing-data disclosure, not evidence of clean accounts. The dossier itself notes that filings later shown to be falsified can appear compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns that problem when it is eligible.

The radar excludes 21 stale institutions rather than presenting them as current. This is selective monitored coverage of institutions with fresh vetted public dossiers, not a universal ranking of lenders.

## What would change the call

A newer vetted filing could weaken or overturn this view if it showed sustained improvement in GNPA while CRAR remained resilient. The pressure chain would be broken if impaired assets improved, credit costs ceased absorbing capital capacity, and reliable liability disclosures showed no associated funding deterioration.

The thesis would strengthen if GNPA deteriorated, CRAR moved toward the licensing floor, or newly eligible evidence revealed deposit, wholesale, CD, or LCR strain. Future market evidence should enter only after rights, identity, timing, model, and tier-use gates are satisfied. A price observation without those authorities is not a substitute for evidence.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the chain from GNPA to provisioning and earnings, from earnings to capital absorption, and from capital capacity to lending flexibility and creditor confidence. Funding is the next link, but it is unlit rather than benign. The relevant research boundary is **institution and lender balance-sheet risk**: that is LiquiLens’s remit.

System conditions belong elsewhere. Seiche addresses **system dollar-funding capacity**, while Undertow addresses **market liquidity and executable exit capacity**.
