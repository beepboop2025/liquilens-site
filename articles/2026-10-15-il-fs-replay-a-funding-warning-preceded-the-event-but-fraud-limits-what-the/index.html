IL&FS replay: a funding warning preceded the event, but fraud limits what the screen could know

IL&FS’s disclosed funding lens registered before the recorded default, while no PCA action-zone entry appears in the replay. That is a filing-period signal under a proxy clock, not a market-price signal, a forecast, or proof of causation.

1395 words · institution risk · open evidence

This historical replay is not current news and not a forecast.

This is a historical replay, not current news, a forecast, or investment advice. IL&FS’s recorded default date is 2018-08-28. The contested proposition is narrow: within the disclosed data used here, IL&FS’s funding lens registered before any recorded formal action-zone entry. It does not follow that default was inevitable, that the signal revealed the full cause, or that an investor had a tradable warning.

The record carries an overriding qualification: IL&FS is fraud-masked. A balance-sheet screen can process disclosed series; it cannot recover facts absent from, or distorted in, those disclosures. The distinction between a filing-period signal, a market-price signal, and a model derivation is therefore central rather than semantic.

The record before the event

IL&FS is classified as an NBFC. Its funding lens first registered a watch-band reading at the 2015-03-31 period end, with an index of 45.4 and no listed flags. The replay assigns a knowledge-time proxy of 2015-05-30, yielding a recorded lead of 38 months to default.

That date is not a verified observation timestamp. The construction rule uses an explicit publication clock where one exists; otherwise it applies period end plus 60 days. This makes 2015-05-30 an availability convention, not evidence that every observer received, interpreted, or could have acted on the disclosure then. The historical evidence is explicitly labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT, lacks a bitemporal input contract, and warns that lead times are optimistic.

The PCA record is different: IL&FS has no recorded first action zone and no PCA lead time. That absence cannot establish that supervisory measures were irrelevant. It may mean that no public action-zone event was captured; it may also reflect missing or misleading public evidence. Fraud masking makes both explanations live possibilities.

What the lenses saw

The funding lens saw a filing-derived vulnerability: a watch-band index in a disclosed liability series. It did not see a market-price confirmation. The market layer is a separate data product, sourced from Yahoo Finance daily closes, and its readings are not authorised for publication, model gates, signals, or tiers in this dossier. Freshness alone is not authority: admitted canonical records are 0/25, while display and tier use remain unapproved. See the market evidence record.

Nor is the filing reading itself a calibrated failure probability. The dossier says PD levels are corpus-fitted and prior-corrected to a 0.25%/yr population base rate, while the Merton PD is a market repricing signal rather than a calibrated failure frequency. The IL&FS watch reading is therefore neither a quoted market judgment nor a claim that a specified probability of failure was known.

Across the historical funding summary, 15 institutions failed, 10 had usable liability disclosures, and the funding signal fired first in 4 cases. Median lead was 38 months. Those results support investigation of disclosed funding structures, but not universal coverage: the lens can only operate where a liability series is available. The historical validation record retains that eligibility boundary.

A broader India diagnostic covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. But it is also construction-PIT, not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and not bitemporal. Those are limits on the result, not boilerplate beneath it.

Why the warning mattered

A funding warning matters because liabilities can require continuing confidence and refinancing while assets may not convert into cash on matching terms. A disclosed liability pattern can therefore merit scrutiny before a formal action-zone measure appears in a public record.

For IL&FS, the defensible conclusion is limited: the funding lens identified its earliest designated disclosed stress evidence at the 2015-03-31 period end, using the 2015-05-30 proxy clock. It was earlier than any recorded PCA action-zone entry because the record contains none. That does not prove the funding signal caused the subsequent default or that it was sufficient to diagnose the institution.

Fraud masking is decisive to interpretation. The PCA summary states that filings later shown falsified can look compliant to a threshold engine, and that the forensic screen owns those cases. A compliant-looking disclosed ratio is not proof of a sound underlying balance sheet. Equally, a visible funding warning can be partial, distorted, or incidental to facts the disclosed series could not show.

The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat the thesis. The apparent 38-month lead may largely be a construction artefact. A period-end plus 60-day proxy is not a complete historical information set, and the dossier explicitly calls lead times optimistic. A full bitemporal archive could shift the availability date, alter the observed sequence, or remove the apparent advantage.

Coverage is a second challenge. Only 10 of 15 failed institutions had usable liability disclosures. A lens that excludes institutions without that series cannot establish that funding risk ranks reliably across all institutions. Missing data must remain missing; it cannot be recoded as a healthy balance sheet.

Third, IL&FS is fraud-masked. The screen may have detected a real disclosed vulnerability, but it cannot establish whether that vulnerability was the dominant mechanism, nor reconstruct unavailable facts. The missing PCA lead is likewise not evidence that PCA-type conditions were absent. The thesis survives only as a prompt to examine disclosed funding, not as a model victory.

But market-derived tier authority is absent here. A market price may be fresh and still be unusable for a published institution tier when admission and display rights are not approved.

The next falsifiable test

The proposition fails if prospectively clocked disclosures show that funding-watch readings do not precede independently established stress more reliably than no such reading, after fraud-masked cases and missing liability series are handled explicitly. It also fails if a complete bitemporal record materially changes the IL&FS ordering.

A credible test would retain exact publication times, preserve unavailable liability series as missing, and test the funding lens against formal action-zone and forensic screens rather than blending them. The existing hazard exercise is insufficient for promotion: its temporal AUC is 0.645, below the 0.65 diagnostic gate, and construction-PIT diagnostics cannot promote.

Follow the pressure chain

The chain begins with disclosed liabilities, then asks whether refinancing and liquidity-management capacity become vulnerable, and only then considers whether mismatched asset and liability timing could constrain payment capacity. IL&FS supplies an early filing-period link in that chain. It does not supply every intervening fact, and fraud masking prevents a complete causal reconstruction.

LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. Undertow covers market liquidity and executable exit capacity. These are adjacent questions, not interchangeable evidence. The IL&FS replay, research archive, and investigations archive provide the appropriate institution-level handoff; the Seiche overview and Undertow board are context, not substitutes for it.

Sources, method, and limits

This article uses the Failure Radar board, validation endpoint, and market evidence endpoint, alongside the LiquiLens replay archive. The relevant historical signal period end is 2015-03-31; its availability proxy is 2015-05-30.

The historical replay is construction-PIT, not a validated backtest or real-money evidence. The market layer is fresh by its own clock but lacks admission, publication, and tier authority. Fraud-masked, missing, stale, future-dated, and unclocked evidence are limitations—not reassurance. The appropriate conclusion is disciplined: IL&FS makes disclosed funding worth investigating early, while showing why public balance-sheet screens cannot claim to see what the records do not reveal.
