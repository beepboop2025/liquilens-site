This is a historical replay, not current news and not a forecast. Abhyudaya Co-operative Bank’s recorded default date is 2023-11-24. The narrow proposition is that its first disclosed RBI action-zone breach was an asset-quality signal that appeared before that event. It is not proof that the breach caused the outcome, that the signal was tradable, or that another institution with a similar disclosure will fail.

Three categories must remain separate. A filing-period signal records what a disclosed balance-sheet measure showed for a reporting period. A market-price signal records repricing in traded instruments. A model derivation turns inputs into a score, tier or diagnostic. They may point in the same direction, but they do not share an observation clock, an evidentiary status or a causal meaning. This article concerns the first category: a disclosure-based replay.

LiquiLens covers **institution and lender balance-sheet risk**. It is not a credit rating and this is not investment advice. The supporting record is available at the [Abhyudaya replay](https://liquilens.in/replay/abhyudaya-co-operative-bank/) and the [Failure Radar board](https://api.liquilens.in/api/failure-radar/board).

## The record before the event

The first recorded action-zone entry was for the period ending 2021-03-31, identified as FY2021. The recorded breach was NNPA. The replay applies a knowledge-time proxy of 2021-05-30: an explicit publication clock when present, otherwise period end plus a 60-day filing-lag proxy. On that convention, the interval to the recorded default date is 29 months.

That is a constructed chronology, not a preserved contemporaneous market record. The historical evidence is labelled PERIOD_END_PROXY_CONSTRUCTION_PIT. It is not validated-backtest eligible, not real-money eligible, and does not have a bitemporal input contract. The dossier explicitly says lead times are optimistic. A period-end measure can be economically relevant before it is publicly available; conversely, a proxy availability date cannot demonstrate when every creditor, depositor or market participant understood its significance.

Abhyudaya is not fraud-masked in this record. Its funding lens was scoreable, but it has no first funding signal. That leaves the NNPA breach as the earliest recorded signal in this replay, not as confirmation that funding conditions were calm or that the asset-quality measure contained the entire explanation.

## What the lenses saw

The PCA/SAF lens saw an NNPA breach. The funding lens did not fire first. That asymmetry is the case’s usable observation: this disclosure framework identified stress first on the asset side.

The broader PCA summary includes 15 failed institutions. Five entered an action zone first, with median lead of 41 months. The funding summary also includes 15 failed institutions, but liability disclosures exist for 10; a funding signal fired first for four, with median lead of 38 months. These are replay descriptions, not failure probabilities, and they do not establish that one lens should always lead another.

The India evidence index covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. That headline carries the same construction-PIT qualification. The [validation record](https://api.liquilens.in/api/failure-radar/validation) reports a temporal AUC of 0.645 against a 0.65 gate. The gate did not pass; construction-PIT diagnostics cannot promote.

A model derivation adds another layer of restraint. The hazard panel contains 205 rows, nine events and 27 institutions, after 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Its leave-one-institution-out AUC is 0.752, while the heuristic comparison is 0.799 on the same held-out rows. Those figures describe a limited diagnostic exercise. They do not validate a live ranking, and they do not improve the evidentiary status of Abhyudaya’s filing-period breach.

## Why the warning mattered

An NNPA breach can matter because asset impairment may reduce a lender’s tolerance for further deterioration. It can make earnings, capital, funding confidence and management options more consequential. But the ratio itself is not a mechanism that mechanically causes default. The replay supports a sequence—disclosed asset-quality stress before the recorded event—not a complete causal account.

The published quadrant rule treats any RBI action-zone breach as red. That is a rule-based response to a supervisory threshold, rather than a claim that a fused score is more authoritative than the underlying breach. In Abhyudaya’s case, the rule directs attention to a disclosed constraint on the balance sheet.

The warning therefore mattered as a scrutiny trigger. It said the institution’s asset side had crossed a stated threshold. It did not say when a final event would occur, whether a market instrument should reprice, or whether the liability side would become the immediate source of pressure.

## The strongest counter-case

The strongest counter-case can defeat the thesis: the apparent lead may be largely a hindsight artefact created by proxy dating. Where no explicit publication clock exists, the record uses period end plus 60 days. The dossier calls the resulting lead times optimistic. A later adverse event can make an earlier ratio look more decisive and more actionable than it was in real time.

There is also no independent market-price confirmation available for publication. The market layer is fresh and clocked, but admitted canonical records are 0/25. Rights, display, model-gate and tier-use approvals are absent, and the layer has no admission, publication or tier authority. It must not be used to infer whether securities markets confirmed, anticipated or rejected the filing-period signal.

Nor does the absence of a funding signal resolve that uncertainty. The funding lens sees only institutions with liability disclosures. Missingness is not reassurance. The dossier also warns that institutions whose filings were later shown falsified can appear compliant to a threshold engine; the forensic screen owns those cases.

## The next falsifiable test

The thesis weakens if prospectively preserved filings with actual publication times show that comparable asset-quality action-zone entries do not provide earlier or more informative warning than other lenses. It weakens as well if timely liability disclosures reliably lead such breaches in the relevant population.

The necessary test is prospective rather than reconstructed: retain original filing vintages, record actual availability times, preserve missingness, and compare the first signal with later stress without letting amended history rewrite the information set. Until that record exists, Abhyudaya is a disciplined historical clue, not a validated forecast.
