New India Co-operative Bank: a disclosed NNPA warning preceded the event, but fraud limits the replay

The reconstructed record places an NNPA action-zone breach before New India Co-operative Bank’s recorded event. That is a filing-period observation, not market-price confirmation or a model-derived failure forecast, and fraud masking limits what the case can establish.

1580 words · institution risk · open evidence

This historical replay is not current news and not a forecast.

This historical replay is not current news, a forecast, a credit rating, or investment advice. New India Co-operative Bank has a recorded default date of 2025-02-13. Its first PCA/SAF action-zone observation belongs to the 2021-03-31 reporting period, labelled FY2021, with a knowledge-time proxy of 2021-05-30.

Those dates have separate meanings. The reporting period describes when the underlying filing-period measure was recorded. The knowledge-time proxy estimates when the information could have become available under the replay’s construction rule. The default date is the recorded outcome. Conflating them would turn a historical reconstruction into a claim about contemporaneous knowledge or tradable timing that the dossier does not support.

The narrow finding is that the reconstructed public record contains an NNPA breach before the recorded event. The dossier records no first funding signal. Neither finding establishes causation, and the fraud-masked status means a ratio-based reconstruction cannot be treated as a complete account of the institution’s condition.

The record before the event

The Failure Radar board identifies New India Co-operative Bank’s first action-zone signal in FY2021. The named breach is NNPA, with a stated lead of 44 months to the recorded event.

This is a filing-period signal. It means a disclosed measure crossed a PCA/SAF-style tripwire in the reconstructed record. It does not show when management understood the underlying condition, when a supervisor acted, when depositors altered behaviour, or when any market incorporated information. Nor does it establish that the NNPA breach caused the event.

The 2021-05-30 date requires particular care. The availability rule uses an explicit publication clock when one is present; otherwise it uses period end plus 60 days. For this case, the date is a knowledge-time proxy generated by that rule, not a verified case-specific publication timestamp. Treating 2021-03-31 as the moment an investor could have acted would overstate the evidence.

Fraud masking is not a footnote. The dossier states that institutions whose filings were later shown falsified can appear compliant to a threshold engine, while the forensic screen addresses that problem. A detected breach therefore does not prove that the engine observed the whole condition, or that the disclosed series captured the decisive source of stress. It is the earliest recorded action-zone breach in this replay.

What the lenses saw

The PCA/SAF lens saw NNPA. The funding lens records no first signal. That is non-firing or unavailable evidence, not affirmative evidence that liabilities were stable. The funding lens only sees institutions that disclose a liability series; lack of a recorded funding signal cannot be converted into a finding of benign funding.

The historical summaries retain that eligibility distinction. Of 15 failed institutions, 5 first entered an action zone, with a median stated lead of 41 months. Of the same 15 failures, 10 had liability disclosures; 4 had a funding signal fire first, with a median stated lead of 38 months. These describe different eligible subsets. They are not probabilities for this bank and do not determine which lens must lead in every failure.

A market-price signal is not part of the case thesis. The current market layer is sourced from Yahoo Finance daily closes, auto-adjusted, but no market-price reading is presented for this institution. More importantly, the pack has no admission, publication, or tier authority: admitted canonical records are 0/25. A fresh retrieval clock does not authorize market-derived institutional conclusions. The market evidence index cannot remove that boundary.

A model derivation is different again. A board tier is a published rule over published components, rather than an independently observed balance-sheet fact. The dossier says the Merton PD is a market repricing signal, not a calibrated failure frequency. It also says the conformal alarm is diagnostic and has no score or tier authority pending prospective revalidation. Those constructions do not change the case-level observation: the disclosed NNPA lens fired, while the funding lens did not record a first signal.

Why the warning mattered

The warning mattered because it located visible, disclosed asset-quality pressure before the recorded event. Under the quadrant rule, any RBI action-zone breach can place an institution in red. That is an operational classification rule, not a declaration that failure was inevitable.

The absence of a recorded funding signal cannot negate the observed impairment evidence. Doing so would mistake a disclosure and eligibility limitation for a positive balance-sheet judgement. The dossier supplies no basis for that inference.

The defensible conclusion is therefore limited: the replay found an early disclosed impairment warning in a fraud-masked case. It did not establish complete detection. Fraud masking explains why the reconstruction can be informative and inadequate at once: it may identify a visible fragment without showing that the disclosure record faithfully represented the full condition.

The wider India diagnostic cannot be used to inflate this single case. It covers 48 institutions across two decades and reports 88.9% of non-fraud failures flagged, with median lead of 21.5 months. Yet it is a filing-availability-proxied construction-PIT diagnostic, is not validated-backtest eligible, is not real-money eligible, and lacks a bitemporal input contract. Its stated lead times are optimistic.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case defeats a stronger thesis. NNPA may be a broad marker of disclosed deterioration rather than a specific explanation for this institution’s outcome. The supplied evidence contains no causal decomposition, liability-run chronology, supervisory narrative, or complete forensic account. It can show only that NNPA was the first recorded PCA/SAF signal, not that it was the first underlying stress.

Fraud intensifies that objection. If disclosures were falsified, the apparent 44-month lead could be an early visible fragment, a delayed representation of deeper stress, or a measure distorted by the condition under examination. The dossier does not allow a choice among these explanations.

Model diagnostics provide no escape from that uncertainty. The hazard panel has 205 rows and 9 events, while 179 censored or unusable rows were excluded. Its temporal diagnostic AUC is 0.645, below the 0.65 gate, and is diagnostic only. The leave-one-institution-out row AUC is 0.752, with an interval from 0.338 to 1.0; the same held-out comparison gives the heuristic an AUC of 0.799. These results argue against prospective promotion, not for a failure forecast. See the historical validation record.

The market layer is available and fresh as of 2026-09-15, but rights and display approvals remain incomplete. It has no publication or tier authority. A market-price reading must be observed and authorized for the claimed use before it can support a current institutional conclusion. That condition is not met.

The next falsifiable test

The thesis would weaken if prospectively clocked filings showed that action-zone NNPA breaches did not precede independently documented stress after fraud-masked cases were separated and liability disclosures were consistently available. It would also weaken if a comparable prospective sample showed the funding lens firing earlier and more reliably than the impairment lens.

The needed test is not another period-end reconstruction. It requires fixed eligibility rules, a bitemporal input contract, verified publication times, and no retrospective substitution of amended or later-known facts. Until then, this remains a bounded historical diagnostic rather than an investable signal.

Follow the pressure chain

Start with the filing-period observation: NNPA crossed the action-zone screen in FY2021. Then identify what is not observed: no first funding signal is recorded, but funding strength is not established. Next separate market evidence: no authorized market-price signal is used. Finally separate model output: a tier or PD is a derivation subject to authority and eligibility rules, not a replacement for the underlying record.

The pressure chain breaks at record trustworthiness. In this fraud-masked case, a ratio engine can assess only the information it receives. The case replay is useful as evidence of that constraint, not as proof that disclosed metrics can see through compromised information.
